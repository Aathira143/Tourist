TEMPLE

Kerala's temple history, spanning over 2,000 years, is defined by unique Dravidian-influenced architecture, profound folklore, and dedication to Vishnu and Shiva. Primarily, ancient legends attribute many temples to Sage Parasurama, while historical records show three distinct construction phases from 800–1800 AD. These temples are renowned for their traditional timber-based structures and rich cultural heritage. Mythological Origins: Many temples, such as the Panniyur Varahamurthy Temple, are believed to have been established by Sage Parasurama over 4,000 years ago. Sree Padmanabhaswamy Temple: Known as the world's richest temple, this 17th-century structure is built on a site with legends dating back 5,000 years to the start of Kali Yuga. In 2011, vast treasures including gold, diamonds, and ancient idols were discovered in its vaults. Ancient Rituals: Temples in Kerala follow unique rituals, with many having their history tied to local legends rather than strictly documented records. The Royal Connection: King Marthanda Varma of Travancore significantly influenced temple administration, introducing ceremonies like Murajapam and Lakshadeepam in the 18th century. Cultural Significance: Kerala temples are not just places of worship but hubs of art, with intricate architecture and traditional art forms like Kathakali and Koodiyattam, often performed as part of festivals.
