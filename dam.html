Idukki Dam: A 168.91-meter-high double-curvature arch dam across the Periyar River. It is one of Asia's highest arch dams and crucial for hydroelectric power. Banasura Sagar Dam: Located in Wayanad, this is the largest earthen dam in India and second largest in Asia, popular for speed boating and trekking. Mullaperiyar Dam: A 129-year-old masonry gravity dam on the Periyar River. Though located in Kerala, it is operated by Tamil Nadu, often causing inter-state debate over its safety. Malampuzha Dam: Located in Palakkad, it is a major dam providing irrigation and drinking water. Cheruthoni Dam: Located close to the Idukki Arch Dam, it is a key component of the Idukki Hydroelectric Project. Hydroelectric Power & Irrigation: Many dams are designed for both, with the Idukki and Idamalayar dams accounting for nearly 48% of the state's total water storage capacity. Tourism: Dams like Thenmala (Ecotourism), Mangalam, and Vazhani are developed as popular tourist spots with boating and parks. Storage: Kerala's dams are frequently monitored by the Kerala State Electricity Board (KSEB) and the Kerala Irrigation Department. Dams are engineering structures built across rivers to retain water, forming reservoirs that facilitate hydroelectric power generation, irrigation, flood control, water supply, and navigation. They are built using concrete, earth, or rock, and can be categorized as gravity, arch, or buttress structures. While vital for resource management, they also cause significant environmental impacts, including habitat fragmentation and greenhouse gas emissions from trapped sediment. Britannica Britannica +4 Key Aspects of Dam Information: Primary Functions: Hydroelectric Power: Generating clean energy. Irrigation: Supplying water for agricultural needs. Flood Control: Managing water flow to prevent floods. Water Supply: Storing water for domestic and industrial consumption. Recreation: Enabling boating, fishing, and tourism. Types of Dams: Gravity Dam: Relies on its own weight to resist water pressure. Arch Dam: Curved structure that transfers water pressure to the canyon walls. Buttress Dam: A watertight upstream face supported by buttresses. Embankment Dam: Built from compacted earth or rock. Major Global Examples: Hoover Dam (USA): A significant concrete arch-gravity dam, known for its massive scale and hydroelectric generation. Itaipu Dam (Brazil-Paraguay): A major producer of hydroelectric energy. Three Gorges Dam (China): A large-scale project focusing on flood control and power generation.

Dams in India: India has over 5,334 large dams, ranking third globally behind China and the USA. Key examples include the Tehri Dam (highest) and Bhakra Dam. The Jawa Dam in Jordan is recognized as one of the oldest known dams, dating to 3000 BC. Environmental and Social Impact: Dams can destroy fish habitats and hinder migration. Reservoir sedimentation can produce methane, contributing up to 1.3% of annual global GHG emissions. They can displace communities and destroy surrounding ecosystems.
